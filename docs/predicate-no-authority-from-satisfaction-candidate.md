## The seam

A ZK proof / credential / reputation score establishes *"this subject satisfies
predicate P."* It does **not** establish *"P is a witnessed, admissible
discriminant fit for this decision."* Privacy hides the subject; it does not
legitimate the category. `signed ≠ witnessed`, applied to predicates.

## Standing's role and refusal

Standing is where an **admitted** predicate becomes usable authority — scoped and
expiring. Its refusal in this doctrine is exactly its existing one, one substrate
out:

> **No authority from predicate satisfaction alone.** A proof that a subject
> satisfies P does not authorize P. Standing converts an *admitted* predicate
> (declared → witnessed → admitted-for-this-use) into authority; it refuses to
> mint authority directly from a satisfaction proof.

This is the same move the clock-witness candidate fences against: the moment
Standing becomes a pure predicate over a proof — *"satisfies P ⟹ may act"* — it
becomes gameable by whoever authored P, and the contextual judgment Standing
exists to hold evaporates. A satisfaction proof is *evidence*, admitted upstream
(Wicket) only against a witnessed predicate; it is never itself the grant.

Chain: declaration → witness → admission → **standing** → spend, with
`admission ≠ standing` and **proof-satisfaction ≠ predicate-authority** the links
Standing owns. Cite the AG canonical note for the full chain and plane map. No
Standing change is implied; this is a handle for review.
